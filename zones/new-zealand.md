# New Zealand – Rare & Endemic Creatures

New Zealand (Aotearoa) has extraordinary endemism, especially among birds, reptiles, amphibians and invertebrates that evolved in isolation.

## Extremely rare / ancient lineages
- **Archey’s frog** (*Leiopelma archeyi*), **Hamilton’s frog** (*L. hamiltoni*), **Hochstetter’s frog** (*L. hochstetteri*) – Living fossils. Belong to a lineage that split from all other frogs ~200 million years ago. Tiny populations, mostly on predator-free islands or remote forests.
- **Kākāpō** (*Strigops habroptilus*) – World’s only flightless parrot. Critically endangered; carefully managed on predator-free islands.
- **Takahē** (*Porphyrio hochstetteri*) – Once thought extinct, rediscovered. Large flightless rail, still very rare.

## Island endemics & recently described
- **Hoplodactylus tohu** (te mokomoko a Tohu) – Gecko “hidden in plain sight” on Cook Strait islands. Separated from relatives for over 5 million years.
- **Manawatāwhi pāua** (*Haliotis pirimoana*) – New abalone species found only around the Three Kings Islands.
- **Manawataawhiorchestia uruone** – New genus and species of landhopper from Three Kings Islands.
- Yellow-eyed penguin (*Megadyptes antipodes*) – Recently shown to consist of three distinct subspecies, all rare.

## Notes
Many more undescribed landhoppers, geckos and invertebrates exist. Conservation relies heavily on predator-free islands.
