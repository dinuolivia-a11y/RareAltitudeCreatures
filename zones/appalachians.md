# Appalachians – 10 Rare High-Elevation Creatures

1. **Spruce-fir moss spider** (*Microhexura montivaga*) – Tiny tarantula relative (3–4 mm). Only in damp moss under Fraser firs on a few peaks. Critically endangered.
2. **Carolina northern flying squirrel** (*Glaucomys sabrinus coloratus*) – Extremely rare subspecies. Fewer than 500 estimated. High ridgelines only.
3. **Appalachian cottontail** (*Sylvilagus obscurus*) – Specialized to high rocky ridgelines. Genetically distinct from common cottontail.
4. **Boreal owl** – Rare bird closely tied to old-growth high spruce-fir forests.
5. **Three-toed woodpecker** – Another specialist of high-elevation spruce-fir stands.
6. **Northern flying squirrel** relatives and high-elevation subspecies – Restricted to shrinking forest islands.
7. Specialized high-elevation salamanders of the southern Appalachians.
8. Rare moths and insects associated with Fraser fir and red spruce.
9. High-ridge endemic ground beetles and micro-arthropods.
10. Various rare songbirds of the high Appalachian peaks.

## Notes
High-elevation Appalachian endemics are often restricted to shrinking spruce-fir islands.
